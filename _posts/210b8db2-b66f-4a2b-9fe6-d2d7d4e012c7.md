# Background  
Forest Creek Capital (FCC) is involved in electricity trading in various geographies in the US. Electricity is traded just like other commodities in various specialized exchanges. These are called regional transmission organizations (RTOs) and Independent System Operators (ISOs) in the US. Unlike other commodities, electricity cannot be stored easily. Hence the demand and supply of electricity has to exactly match at every moment. Because of this, the electricity price is very volatile. Many exchanges have implemented a system of day-ahead and real-time markets.
Day-Ahead Market: This market involves the buying and selling of electricity one day in advance. Prices are determined based on forecasted demand, generation availability, and transmission constraints. This market produces one price (day ahead LMP) for every location or node for every hour of the next day.
Real-Time Market: This market operates closer to the actual time of electricity delivery, adjusting for any deviations from the Day-Ahead forecasts due to unexpected changes in demand or generation. The Real-Time Energy Market produces a separate, second financial settlement. It establishes the real-time locational marginal price (LMP) that is either paid or charged to participants in the Day-Ahead Energy Market for demand or generation that deviates from the day-ahead commitments.
 
FCC buys and sells electricity in the day-ahead market and do the opposite trade in the real-time market. Hence their profit depends on correctly predicting the difference between the day-ahead and real-time market prices.
FCC has gathered hourly day-ahead and real-time prices for tens of thousands of locations in the US for over a decade. They also have related data like electricity demand and wind energy output.

# Project Objective
Using time series modeling, predict the day ahead energy prices for a small set of selected locations. Use related data like forecasted and actual demand at various locations and wind power output for this prediction. Use meaningful measure of accuracy e.g. mean absolute error and root mean square error

Upon further reading, I learned that price is more closely related to ‘net load’ which is electricity demand minus wind generation. The way electricity market works is that the marginal generator bids sets the prices. All generator owners submit their offers in the market. Depending on the forecasted demand and forecasted renewable generation output, the offer curves of the generators are arranged in ascending order. The price where the generator capacity matches the net load is the price paid by all the users and this is the price that every generator receives in that hour. The situation is a little different when transmission lines are congested but this simplistic idea still explains prices to a large degree.

Next Steps: There are a lot of experiments which can be performed to improve the accuracy of this forecast. We can try using forecasted net load as exogenous variable.
