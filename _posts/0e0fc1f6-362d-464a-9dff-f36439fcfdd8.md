# Introduction
I liked advanced math concepts from an early age. I was looking for an opportunity to do a summer camp to learn some advance math skills and came across the CTY program. Getting acceptance was not easy as I had to score well on a higher level PSAT test in order to be admitted. 
# The Camp
The Center of Talented Youth (CTY) program at Johns Hopkins University was an intense and intellectually stimulating experience that took place on the beautiful UC Santa Cruz campus in California. 

Over the course of three weeks, I immersed myself in a course on Probability and Game Theory, a subject that has always fascinated me.

The curriculum was both challenging and rewarding, pushing me to explore complex mathematical concepts and their real-world applications. The small class size allowed for deep discussions and personalized feedback from our instructor, who was incredibly knowledgeable and passionate about the subject. Outside of class, I enjoyed exploring the campus and bonding with fellow students who shared my enthusiasm for learning.

The CTY program not only strengthened my mathematical skills but also introduced me to a community of talented individuals who inspired me to continue pursuing my academic interests with greater determination.

# Course Description:

The study of probability and game theory allows students to apply math to real-world situations. In this course, you’ll learn to use some of the major tools of game theory, a branch of mathematics focused on the application of mathematical reasoning to competitive behavior. You’ll explore concepts like dominance, mixed strategies, utility theory, Nash equilibria, and n-person games, and learn how to use tools from probability and linear algebra to analyze and develop successful game strategies. The games you’ll “play” in this course are abstract representations of real-life situations; for example, Nash equilibria have been used to solve questions of political competition and analyze penalty kicks in soccer, and the Prisoner’s Dilemma has been used to examine the social networks of different populations. Class exercises involve individual and group work and may also include fun class tournaments.
